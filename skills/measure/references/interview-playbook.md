# Interview playbook

How to draw out what someone actually did. Pitch questions in plain words, one at a time, and build each on their last answer.

## Probing moves

- **Example before adjective.** "Good communicator", "strong leader", "fast learner" are claims, not facts. Ask: "Can you tell me about a time that showed it?"
- **Personal contribution.** If they say "we", ask what they themselves did.
- **The counterfactual.** "What would have happened if you hadn't done that?" surfaces impact people take for granted.
- **Chase a number, never suggest one.** Ask "Is there a figure that goes with that, even roughly?" If there isn't, leave `metric` out.
- **Turn a vague amount into a figure or a bound.** "Several thousand", "a lot of users", "a big saving": ask "Roughly how many, or what is the least it could have been?" A bound such as "at least 2,000" is fine; an adjective is not.
- **Name the stack.** For each piece of work ask "What did you build it with?" and put the answer in `tech`: languages, datastores, brokers, cloud services, tools. Ask per fact, because a skill used in one role is not evidence for another.
- **Size of your part, in verbs.** If they say "a big part of" or "a major influence", ask "What did you personally do?" and record that instead.
- **Leading people.** For any role where they lead a team, ask one question at a time: do they line-manage anyone (how many), or lead technically with no reports; how big is the team and who is in it; and what do they still do hands-on. Record `management` and `hands_on`.
- **Process and decisions.** Ask how they went about it and what they chose not to do, because that is what employers read as competence.
- **Stay in their words.** Keep their scope words and their terms for tools and methods; later skills mirror a job ad's wording only where it is true.

## Exploration rounds

Offer each round on its own. Each is optional and skippable; a skip is accepted without pushback. Use two or three seed questions, not all of them.

### 1. Proudest and hardest moments
Purpose: the best material, which often is not on the CV.
- What are you proudest of from your working life, and why?
- What was the hardest stretch, and what got you through it?

### 2. Beyond the job description
Purpose: unofficial responsibilities that show initiative.
- What did you do that wasn't part of your role, but needed doing?
- Did you set anything up, fix anything, or teach anyone without being asked?

### 3. Things that went wrong
Purpose: judgement and learning.
- Tell me about something that didn't go to plan. What did you change afterwards?
- Is there a decision you'd make differently now?

### 4. How you work
Purpose: process, tools, trade-offs.
- Walk me through how you usually start a new piece of work.
- How do you decide what to do first when everything is urgent?

### 5. People
Purpose: leading, mentoring, influence.
- Who have you led, mentored or onboarded?
- Tell me about a difficult stakeholder and how you handled them.

### 6. Recognition
Purpose: external evidence.
- What have colleagues or managers praised you for?
- Any promotions, awards, or things people come to you for?

### 7. Transferable skills
Purpose: especially for people changing direction.
- Which parts of your work would carry over to a different field?
- What do you do well that isn't in your job title?

### 8. What you want next
Purpose: fills `preferences`.
- What kind of role do you want next, and what would you rather avoid?
- Where would you like to work, and in what setup?

## Wrap-up questions

After the rounds, list thin areas: roles with no outcomes, facts with no numbers or only vague amounts, facts with no `tech`, leading roles with no `management`, skills that no fact names, a `profile` with no city or no phone, and roles with very few facts. Offer to dig into any of them now or on a later run.
